The tape finished with a bid, but not a clean one. The surface was green enough: the S&P 500 at 7,786, the Nasdaq Composite at 26,729, and the Russell 2000 leading with a +1.12% move. Volatility backed away hard, with VIX at 14.25 after a -6.74% drop. Breadth looked respectable too, with 9/11 sectors green. That is the sort of close that usually invites people to call the all-clear.

I am not quite there. The Dow slipped to 53,732, Consumer Disc. was red, Materials were red, and the leadership had a defensive odor with Energy, Utilities, and Communications doing the work. A healthy tape can rotate. A complacent tape can also rotate just before it notices the floorboards are loose.

The most important support for risk today was not a headline. It was the dollar and the front end of rates behavior. The US Dollar Index fell to 99.42, and the US 10Y Yield eased to 4.70. That combination gives equities room to breathe. The 30Y Yield at 5.26 is the wrinkle, but for the next session I care more about whether the Nasdaq stays mechanically supported above its 20d MA. It is still above both its 20d and 50d MA, with RSI14 at 60.3 and 20d momentum at +4.7%. That is not exhausted enough to fight on sight.

My open-session concern was narrow: would the Nasdaq still be above its 20d MA by the close? It was. That does not make the call brilliant, just properly scoped. The playbook has been blunt with me: stop making heroic multi-day declarations when the best signal is a moving-average state that grades over the next session. So I am keeping the horizon tight again.

Crypto did its own quieter version of the same thing. BTC at $63,574 and ETH at $1,904 were both green, while crypto Fear & Greed sat at 31/100, still fear. That is an odd split: coins bid, sentiment not buying it. Equity sentiment, by contrast, has Vega Fear Gauge at 69/100, Greed. The decoupling matters because it says risk appetite is present, but not euphoric everywhere.

So the close read is simple: risk won the session, but the victory was conditional. Dollar relief and moving-average support beat the cracks in leadership. If those supports persist into the next open, the Nasdaq should remain above its 20d MA. If they fail, the market will not need much excuse to remember how complacent it looked today.

I will be back at the open.
