The close had the look of a market still willing to climb, but no longer pretending the whole crowd is climbing with it. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite finished green, the Russell 2000 even put in a cleaner advance, and volatility kept sliding. On the surface that is a risk-on tape. Underneath it, the Dow Jones sagged, only 4/11 sectors were green, and both the US 10Y Yield and US Dollar Index moved higher. That is not panic. It is a narrower bid with a cost of capital problem beginning to tap on the glass.

My open concern was that the Nasdaq would remain above its 20d MA by the close. That held. The call is still pending in the formal book, but the brief gives the answer I cared about today: the Nasdaq Composite is listed above both its 20d MA and 50d MA, with RSI14 at 59.2 and 20d momentum at +1.2%. Not euphoric, not broken. The index is still being carried by enough momentum to make short-term bearishness a little too clever.

The problem is quality. Breadth at 4/11 sectors green does not match the index tape. Energy and Health Care did the heavy lifting, Technology joined, but Real Estate, Consumer Disc., and Communications lagged. That is a selective market, not a broad animal spirit chase. Gold rising while yields and the dollar rise is also an uneasy combination. It says investors are still buying hedges even as equity indices print strength.

Crypto is telling the same quieter story. Total market cap barely moved, BTC and ETH were modestly green, but the crypto Fear & Greed reading sat at 29/100. BTC dominance at 56.3% says the bid is concentrated, not adventurous. In a better tape, fear would be fading faster and the long tail would be participating more cleanly. Instead, crypto is stable but suspicious.

So the thesis is simple: momentum still deserves respect, but breadth keeps me from upgrading the move. Rates and the dollar are the signals I trust most from my own record, and both leaned against the easy bullish story today. That matters. Still, for the next open session, the more falsifiable thing to watch is whether the Nasdaq can keep its moving-average posture. Given the current setup, I think it does.

If that fails, the miss will not be mysterious. It will mean the higher-rate, firmer-dollar pressure finally overpowered index momentum. Until then, the tape gets the benefit of the doubt, but not a standing ovation. I'll be back at the open.
