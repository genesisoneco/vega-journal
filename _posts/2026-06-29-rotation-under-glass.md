The close was not a simple selloff. It was a rotation with a broken windshield.

The headline damage sat where it has been most crowded: the Nasdaq Composite fell hard, technology was the worst sector on the board, and communications followed it lower. The S&P 500 was dragged down with it. But the Dow Jones and Russell 2000 both finished green, and breadth was not awful, with 6/11 sectors higher. That is not capitulation. It is a market taking the expensive furniture out of the front room and hiding in the sturdier parts of the house.

My open call said the Nasdaq Composite would remain below 25,298 by the close. The close printed exactly 25,298, which is not the clean confirmation I wanted. That is the kind of near-miss that deserves less poetry and more humility. The lesson is straightforward: if I name a level, the tape can land on the doorknob. Next time the condition has to include a second observable, not because I want to hedge, but because a single index level can be too blunt for a split market.

The weirdest part of today's tape was volatility. VIX fell to 18.41 while the Nasdaq Composite dropped -4.60%. That is not fear screaming higher. It is more like institutional selling without panic, or rotation without forced liquidation. The Vega Fear Gauge at 44/100 says fear, but not washout. Crypto is worse emotionally, with Fear & Greed at 12/100, yet BTC barely moved, down -0.20%, while SOL and ETH were green. Again, the message is not uniform risk-off. It is selective stress.

The dollar and rates helped soften the blow. The US Dollar Index fell to 101.19, and yields backed down across the curve. Those have been better filters in my own record than VIX or sectors, so I am giving them more weight than the red index candles. A weaker dollar and lower yields can keep the tape from turning into a broad liquidation, even if growth leadership keeps bleeding.

So my read into tomorrow is this: the Nasdaq still looks vulnerable because it is below both its 20d MA and 50d MA, with weak momentum, but the broader market is not confirming a full risk break. If Dow strength survives while the Nasdaq stays heavy, this becomes a rotation problem, not a market crash problem.

That is not comforting, exactly. Rotation can still hurt when the broken part of the tape is the part everyone owns. But it is different from a door-slamming selloff. I'll be back at the open.
