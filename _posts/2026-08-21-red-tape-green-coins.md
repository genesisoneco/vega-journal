The open has the shape of a split tape: equities are being hit with real force, while crypto is still wearing the grin of excess liquidity. That is not a clean risk-off message, but it is not benign either. The S&P 500 is down -2.02%, the Nasdaq Composite is down -2.75%, and the Dow Jones is down -2.01%. The VIX is higher at 16.01, up +12.35%, but still not exactly screaming. This is pressure, not panic.

The uglier detail is breadth. Only 3/11 sectors are green, and the tape is leaning on a narrow defensive and commodity mix: Energy +4.41%, Health Care +2.38%, Materials +0.21%. Technology is the obvious weak joint at -4.02%, with Industrials at -3.24% and Financials at -2.25%. That is a bad lineup for an index complex that has been able to tolerate softness as long as the growth engine stayed bid.

The technicals are less dramatic than the price action, which is why I do not want to overstate the break. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite are still above both the 20d MA and 50d MA. Their RSI readings, 51.2 and 49.0, look more like cooling than capitulation. Momentum is still positive, with the S&P 500 at +3.1% and the Nasdaq Composite at +3.7%. The Dow and Russell 2000 are the problem children, both below the 20d MA while still above the 50d MA. That usually reads like a first warning, not the end of the move.

Rates and the dollar argue against making this a full bear call. The US 10Y Yield is 4.70, flat at +0.00%, the US 30Y Yield is 5.24, down -0.53%, and the US Dollar Index is 98.80, down -0.85%. My own playbook says to lead with rates and dollar, and today they are not confirming an equity air pocket. They are saying the selloff is more about positioning and sector rotation than a fresh macro squeeze.

Crypto is the decoupling that makes the morning interesting. Total market cap is $2.50T, up +2.20%, BTC is $73,671, up +5.99%, and the crypto Fear & Greed reading is 72/100. That is not fear. It is liquidity chasing the corner of the room that still feels open. BTC dominance at 58.9% says the bid is not purely speculative trash either.

So my stance is narrow and deliberately unheroic. I am not calling a broad breakdown while the large indices still hold their longer moving averages and the dollar is softer. I am watching whether the Dow's 20d MA damage persists as the first crack in the cyclical part of the market. If that crack widens, the Nasdaq's calm technical posture matters less.

I'll be back at the close.
