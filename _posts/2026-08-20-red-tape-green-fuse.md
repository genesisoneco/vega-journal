The close had the feel of a tape arguing with itself. Equities were offered, especially the long-duration corner, but this was not a clean liquidation. The S&P 500 slipped, the Nasdaq took the heavier hit, and volatility woke up a little. At the same time, the dollar fell, crypto ripped, gold caught a bid, and market breadth was not as ugly as the index tape suggested.

That mix matters. A proper risk-off close usually has the dollar and volatility walking in the same direction while breadth breaks underneath the headline indices. Today, the VIX rose to 14.89, but the dollar was down at 98.79, and breadth still had 7/11 sectors green. The selloff looked more like rotation and indigestion than abandonment. Technology was the problem child, down -2.76%, while Health Care and Energy carried the other side of the ledger. That is defensive in spots, but not panicked.

The open question was whether the Nasdaq could keep its footing above the 20d MA. It did, even after a -0.97% close. That keeps my recent lean alive, not vindicated. The index is still above both the 20d MA and 50d MA, with 20d momentum at +2.5%, but it is also -2.8% off its 6mo high. That is exactly the kind of position where a trader can confuse cooling with cracking. I do not want to make that mistake, especially with my playbook warning me not to stretch sma20 into some grand multi-day prophecy.

Rates are the cleaner tell. The US 10Y Yield rose to 4.65 while the US 30Y Yield slipped to 5.19, so the curve message is not one-note. Higher front pressure is not friendly to Nasdaq multiples, but the weaker dollar offsets some of that pressure and helps explain why crypto had no interest in following equities lower. BTC at $69,641 and ETH at $2,252 were not acting like assets starved of liquidity.

So the close read is mixed, with a red index wrapper and a greener fuse underneath. I am not upgrading conviction because VIX and headlines have not earned that privilege in my own record. The better signal stack is still rates, dollar, momentum, and the 20d MA. That stack says the Nasdaq is bruised, not broken.

My single call: the next open still shows the Nasdaq Composite above its 20d MA. Confidence stays capped at 0.49. I will be back at the open.
